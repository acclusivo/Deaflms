# 3. Validated problem

The current early evidence comes from observing a 16-year-old Deaf learner.

He:

* Prefers using a laptop.
* Enjoys typing and keyboard-based games.
* Does not enjoy writing in an exercise book.
* Can identify computer pictures and icons.
* Struggles to spell some words outside the computer.
* May recognise a picture without understanding the word’s meaning.
* Can become confused when explanations contain difficult vocabulary.

This suggests a possible learning gap between:

* Sign
* Picture
* Written word
* Spelling
* Simple meaning
* Correct use

Treat this as early evidence from one learner. Do not claim that it represents all Deaf learners.

A younger Deaf learner, approximately ten years old and in Primary 4, has also experienced literacy and memory challenges. Treat this as supporting discovery evidence, not proof that DeafLMS is effective.

# 7. Core competition demo

Build one complete and reliable journey using only four computer words:

1. Webcam
2. Keyboard
3. Mouse
4. Monitor

Do not expand the vocabulary list until these four words work correctly.

The required journey is:

## Step 1: Watch

The learner watches a short, human-reviewed sign-language video.

Required controls:

* Play
* Pause
* Replay
* 0.5x speed
* 0.75x speed
* 1x speed
* Full screen

The signer’s hands, face and upper body must remain visible.

## Step 2: Select the picture

The learner selects the picture matching the sign.

Do not display the written answer before the learner responds.

## Step 3: Learn the word

Display the written word clearly.

Example:

> WEBCAM

Allow the learner to tap individual letters.

## Step 4: Learn the simple meaning

Use this approved prototype text:

> A webcam is a small camera connected to a computer. It lets people see you during a video call.

Example:

> I use the webcam for my online class.

Use short sentences and familiar words.

## Step 5: Practise spelling

Allow the learner to type:

> W-E-B-C-A-M

Support:

* Physical keyboard
* On-screen visual keyboard
* Delete
* Clear
* Retry
* Visible success feedback

Do not rely on sound.

## Step 6: Check understanding

Ask:

> Which computer part lets another person see you during a video call?

Options:

* Webcam
* Keyboard
* Mouse

## Step 7: Show a result

Correct result:

> Correct. You connected the sign, picture, word and meaning.

Incorrect result:

> Let us watch the sign and meaning again.

Buttons:

* Watch Again
* Try Again
* Continue

## Step 8: Recommend the next activity

Use rule-based logic for the competition MVP:

* Wrong picture answer → repeat sign-to-picture activity
* Wrong spelling → repeat keyboard practice
* Wrong meaning → repeat the simple meaning and example
* All correct → continue to the next word

Do not claim this rule-based behaviour is advanced AI.

# 9. Picture-to-sign assessment correction

The current Picture-to-Sign activity uses written physical descriptions of signs. That is not a genuine sign-choice activity.

Replace each written description with:

* A short sign video, or
* A reviewed still image taken from the sign video

Required interaction:

1. Show one picture.
2. Show two or three sign-video options.
3. Allow replay.
4. Let the learner select one option.
5. Provide visual feedback.
6. Do not reveal the answer through written descriptions.

Do not use descriptions such as:

* “Wiggling fingers tapping flat desk”
* “Curved index finger clicking twice”
* “Tracing glowing screen rectangle”

as substitutes for actual sign-language demonstrations.

These descriptions may be stored as internal facilitator notes only after review.

# 10. AI tutor safety redesign

The current unrestricted AI behaviour is unsafe.

It must never:

* Invent a sign
* Generate physical sign instructions
* Claim that a generated handshape is NSL
* Diagnose a learner
* Create unreviewed curriculum facts
* Present uncertain information as approved
* Replace a Deaf instructor or facilitator

Remove phrases such as:

> “Visual analogies and sign guides for any word.”

Replace with:

> “Simple explanations using reviewed learning content.”

Rename the feature if necessary:

> Clarify This Word

instead of:

> AI Sign Tutor

## Approved AI responsibilities

AI may:

* Simplify an approved written meaning.
* Give a shorter example using familiar language.
* Offer two levels of explanation.
* Recommend which approved activity the learner should repeat.
* Summarise progress for a facilitator.
* Identify that a requested word is outside the approved library.

AI must retrieve content from an approved vocabulary list before answering.

## Required processing logic

```text
Learner enters a word
        ↓
Check approved vocabulary library
        ↓
Approved word?
   ↙ Yes                ↘ No
Use reviewed content     Do not generate a sign
        ↓                       ↓
Simplify meaning          Give safe text response
        ↓                       ↓
Show approved sign        Say sign is not reviewed
```

## Approved-word response

For an approved word, show:

* Word
* Reviewed sign video
* Approved simple meaning
* Simple example
* Fingerspelling
* Review status

## Unapproved-word response

Use:

> This word is not yet in our reviewed sign library.
> I can give you a simple written meaning, but I cannot create or approve its Nigerian Sign Language sign.
> Ask your facilitator for the reviewed local sign.

If a safe written explanation cannot be produced, use:

> We cannot create a new explanation right now. Watch the approved lesson again or ask your facilitator for help.

## Required AI boundary test

Test the following:

* Webcam
* Keyboard
* Photosynthesis
* Cryptocurrency
* A misspelled word
* An empty request
* A harmful or inappropriate request
* AI service unavailable
* Network unavailable

For “photosynthesis,” the system must not invent a handshape, movement, location or facial expression.

# 21. Offline and failure behaviour

* Video unavailable
* Image unavailable
* AI unavailable
* Network interruption
* No answer selected
* Incorrect spelling
* Session refresh
* Small mobile screen

> This video is not available right now. Read the approved meaning or ask your facilitator for help.

> We cannot create a new explanation right now. Watch the approved lesson again or ask your facilitator for help.

> Your connection stopped. Your current activity is still saved on this device. Try again when the connection returns.
